<p>Einstein back in 1905 wrote a thesis on special relativity.</p>
<p>
<h4>Google search : <b1>in a special relativity experiment, would a stationary observer and a traveller agree when the traveller passes a designated landmark?</b1>
</h4>
<h4>Ai Results</h4>
<p>Yes, both observers will agree that the traveller is passing the landmark at that exact moment, but they will disagree on when (the time on their respective clocks) and where (the distance from a starting point) that event occurs
</p>
<p>In special relativity, this is known as a "local event." Because the traveller and the landmark are at the same location at the same instant, there is no spatial separation between them for that specific event.</p>
<h4>Key Points of Agreement and Disagreement</h4>
<ul>
  <li><b>Coincidence (Agreement)</b> : Both the stationary observer and the traveller agree on the "coincidence"—the physical fact that the traveller is at the landmark</li>
  <li><b>Time (Disagreement)</b> : Due to time dilation, the traveller’s clock will show less elapsed time than the stationary observer’s clock since the start of the trip</li>
  <li><b>Distance (Disagreement)</b> : Due to length contraction, the traveller will measure a shorter distance between the starting point and the landmark than the stationary observer.</li>
</ul>

</p>
<p>

The second hand demonstrates that each frame of reference is syncronized. The astronauts on the spacecraft report the exact time they passed the start of test position marker.

detector display is not a second hand but actually a nanosecond hand, since this is the actual scale of timing flux. The spacial trajactory path is shown by the relative path circles, that are blue
and red. The length difference scale is in nanometers. 
</p>

of the nanosecond hand at the red path radius is slower than the blue path. This shows that the experience both the observatory crew and the 
spacecraft crew is the same. That is, time is synchronized in terms of when shared moments occur, such as the experiment start and finish place and time.
