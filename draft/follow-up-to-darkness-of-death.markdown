On 27 Oct 2027, I was reading Heb 6:7 'for land that has drunk the rain that has fallen on it and produces a crop
useful to those for whose sake it is cultivated receives a blessing from God. But if it bears thorns and thistles
it is worthless and near to being cursed and it's end is to be burned.'

It occurred to me that this verse is using cultivated land as a metaphor of a disciple, and recalled that in
other scripture land is personified. eg about the promised land 'it is a land that vomits out it's inhabitants'.
I wondered then if in the economy of God the territory of a nation and in particular the ecosystem of the 
unpopulated regions is a living creature. To be short, is an ecosystem a living creature in the Lord's view.
To this question, the Lord said 'Yes'.

Refer to Rev 12 which summarises some of the main themes in the current blog series.

1. A woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet and on her head a crown of 12 stars. She was pregnant
and crying out in birth pains and the agony of giving birth. 

2. Another sign appeared, a great red dragon with seven heads and 10 horns and on his head seven diadems. 

The dragon stood before the woman who was about to give birth so that when she bore her child he might devour it.
She gave birth to a male child, one who was to rule the nations with a rod of iron, but her child was caught up to God
and to His throne.

When the dragon saw that he was thrown down to earth, he pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child.
But the woman was given the two wings of a great eagle so that she might fly from the dragon into the wilderness, 
where she was to be nourished for a time, times and half a time.

The serpent poured water like a river out of his mouth after the woman, to sweep her away with a flood. But the 
earth came to the help of the woman by opening its mouth and swallowed the river that dragon had poured out.
Then the dragon was furious and went off to make war on the offspring of the woman, those who keep the commandments
of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus. And he stood on the land and sea.

Themes that appear in Rev 12 :

The woman is a prodigal of God who is called to eventually rule with God. She is clothed with the sun with the moon under her feet, with a crown of twelve stars.
The woman is Israel because she has a crown of twelve stars. She gave birth to Christ, who is the first born from
the dead. Later the dragon makes war against the offspring of the woman who hold to the testimony of Jesus.

1. The Israel of God is a type of person or spirit whose seed is Christ.
2. The spritual heritage of Israel is Christ and the family of God is defined by twelve base branches that connect 
to Christ who is the root of the olive tree.
3. Jesus was received in heaven after His resurrection and commissioned as a priest and king forever in the order
of Melchizedek. this is a prophesied in Leviticus by the ceremony for cleasing a leper, which has the same ritual
as the ordination of a priest combined with the ordination of a king.

4. The serpent has seven heads with seven diadems which is the same number as the lamb who was slain. this indicates
the ultimate goal of Satan, to impersonate and supplant Jesus as the ruler of the universe and the ruler over the
whole creation of God.

5. The serpent poured water out of his mouth to sweep the woman away in a flood.
This is more evidence that the biblical meaning of water is speech, which has power to move a person where ever
the water channel is directed.

6. The mouth is a sword, because the Greek word for mouth may be described as 'a cutting in the face'. the mouth
of the earth is a cutting in the face of the earth. the root word means 'the edge of a sword'. So the help is from
the Word of God which is the Sword of the Spirit. 

7. The eagle is a great aerobird, because the root word of eagle is 'aer' and means 'to fly'. the English word
aeroplane is a flying plane. So a great aerobird must be a aeroplane that can travel to great height and distance.

About Matt 18:7 "Woe to the world for temptations to sin? For it is neccessary for temptations to come but woe to
the one by whom the temptation comes! And if your hand or your foot causes you to sin cut it off and throw it away.
For it is better that you enter life crippled or lame than to have two hands and two feet and be thrown into the 
eternal fire". The Lord is referring to two aspects of Christian life 1. the virtual body of each person and 2.
the tares that are among the wheat. That is, Paul explains the corporate body which is one body with many members
but in reality and in terms of the earthly activity of the church there is not one complete church body but many 
thousands that share the same culture and theology. so when Jesus says 'if your eye causes you to sin gouge it
out, He means 'if the prophetic voice that you trust is not aligned with the gospel then it is your own responsibility
to cut that person off and goes elsewhere'. And the same for the hand and foot 'if your hand is feeding you food
that is leavened by a worldview of sin, then cut off that hand. If your foot is carrying you towards a table of
food that looks great but it tastes of the world then cut off that foot. The hand and foot could be a single person or a 
church movement.

When Jesus said to Peter in Matt 16:18 "I tell you that you are Peter and on this rock I will build my church." The
Lord is not talking about just one person called Peter, but instead He is talking about the name Peter which means
Rock as a type of apostle. Jesus was talking to Peter, who is a prototype apostle. The great apostles of the first
church contributed greatly to building the church and they are all Peters. The Rock is a type of gift that enables
the person to endure great trials without turning back, like Peter, Paul and John.
